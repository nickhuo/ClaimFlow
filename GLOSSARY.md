# Insurance Claims Support

Claims support concerns a policyholder's cases. Caller statements, identity evidence, representative authorization and email consent have different meanings and authority.

## Language

**Subject**:
The policyholder to whom the referenced policy and case belong. When a representative calls, the subject remains the represented policyholder.
_Avoid_: Caller (when referring to case ownership)

**Policyholder**:
The person who holds a policy and has reference identity information that can be checked. A policy number can locate a policyholder but cannot prove the caller's identity on its own.

**Caller**:
The person participating in the current conversation, either a policyholder or a representative.
_Avoid_: Subject (when assuming an unverified caller owns a case)

**Identity evidence**:
The result of actually comparing one identity field against a particular subject's reference information. Caller statements and repeated submissions of the same field do not add distinct evidence.

**Identity verification**:
A conclusion supported by at least three distinct identity fields matching the same subject, with no unresolved conflict. It does not establish representative authorization or email consent.

**Representative relationship**:
A recorded relationship between a representative and a policyholder. The relationship alone grants no access to case information.

**Representative authorization**:
Permission for a verified representative to act for a specified subject within an explicit scope and validity period.
_Avoid_: Relationship (when claiming permission has been granted)

**Case**:
An insurance claim record belonging to one subject, with its type, status, amounts and any available denial, document or appeal information.

**Case hint**:
A caller-supplied case identifier, type, month or status description that helps locate a case. A hint is not a case fact.

**Case facts**:
Case information obtained from trusted records, with unavailable information remaining unknown. Caller assertions do not change case facts.

**Requested document**:
A material item explicitly requested by a case record. A guidance title does not impose an additional original requirement.

**Document type**:
The category of information a document provides; equivalent names may identify the same category. A pathology report and a diagnosis report are different materials, and an original requirement does not change the document type.

**Original requirement**:
An explicit case requirement to provide an original document. An unspecified original requirement remains unknown and cannot be inferred from a guidance title.

**Document guidance**:
Instructions for preparing or submitting requested documents. General suggestions about alternatives do not establish that a particular substitute has been accepted.

**Appeal deadline**:
The original appeal date stated in a case record. An expired date is not automatically extended or replaced by relative advice without a starting point.

**Email consent**:
The caller's explicit choice to send a particular version of a conversation summary to a specified recipient. It does not grant access to case information.

**Handoff**:
A request for a human to continue handling the conversation. A handoff request does not mean a human is already connected or that identity and authorization requirements have been lifted.
